How the Model Works

  • Distance & angle drive the baseline: Every shot is scored by how far it is from the center of the goal (on a log scale so extra meters close to goal matter most) and how wide the open angle is between the two goal posts.
  • Body part shifts the odds: Right-foot and left-foot shots share the same baseline, while headers and other body parts (knee, chest, etc.) have lower expected goals from the exact same spot.
  • Match context adjusts the estimate: Being under defensive pressure lowers xG, and direct free kicks use a separate dead-ball adjustment. First-time strikes have almost no effect in this model.
  • Traffic in front of goal blocks shots: Each defender positioned inside the shooting triangle from the ball to the two goal posts reduces the probability of scoring.
  • Logistic regression turns inputs into a probability: The model adds up the weighted score of all 8 features and passes it through a sigmoid function to produce a probability between 0% and 100% (penalties are not modeled).

Data Source

StatsBomb Open Data

Fitted on 16,164 non-penalty shots (1,484 goals) from StatsBomb Open Data covering major international tournaments and one Premier League season. The logistic regression model was fitted by the author using StatsBomb's 120 × 80 yard coordinate system internally, while distances on screen are displayed in meters (1 yd = 0.9144 m).

Known Limitations

  • Trained on 16,164 shots from major tournaments and one Premier League season, so it reflects those matches only.
  • No goalkeeper position, no shot speed, no assist type. StatsBomb's own xG uses more context and scores better (AUC about 0.79 vs 0.76).
  • Free kicks, and shots from within about 2 yards (1.8 m), are the least reliable estimates.
  • Free-kick xG is overstated unless you set Defenders in cone to about 4 for the wall.
  • Defender count is entered by hand here, while in the data it comes from the freeze frame.
